# Festivals and holidays

Every festival and holiday Occasio can make a card for: 51 of them, from Diwali, Holi and Eid to
Halloween, Christmas and New Year. Picking one starts a normal card (photo, frame, words, share link) with the
festival's greeting, colours, celebration effect and opening lines already set.

## What a person sees

- **Home** puts "Festivals coming up" at the very top, under the greeting: a swipeable row of cards, each with
  the festival, how soon it is ("Tomorrow", "In 12 days", "Yesterday") and its date. Tap a card to start a wish
  for it; "See all" opens the whole list. It shows the widely celebrated festivals (the "On Home" column below) in
  the next 60 days, soonest first, up to six, and keeps yesterday's festival for a late wish. With nothing coming
  up it is just a "Festivals and holidays" link.
- **"What's the moment?"** (the step after choosing who it is for) has the same row above the five everyday
  moments, so a festival is one tap away there too, and it keeps who the card is for.
- **Festivals and holidays** (the full list) opens from "See all". It leads with *Coming up* (every festival in
  the next 60 days, not only the well-known ones) and then lists all of them in groups, each with its date.
- **The card** says "Happy Diwali" on the frame, offers that festival's opening lines in *Say it your way*, and
  on the recipient's page shows "Happy Diwali," above the name, in the festival's colours, with its own effect
  and song. It opens as the interactive story like every other moment (gate, balloons, gift, photos and
  reasons, letter, finale), with the festival's name where the story would say "birthday".
- **Link previews** read "Happy Diwali" over the name, in the festival's colours. The share-your-reaction picture
  uses them too.

A festival is a card type, like `birthday`, so it is stored, searched in History ("new year" finds it), edited
and re-shared the same way, and keeps working after the day has passed.

## Where it lives

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| The list: names, greetings, groups, colours, effects, icons, opening lines | `src/features/create/domain/holidays.ts` |
| Date rules and the moon-festival lists | `src/features/create/domain/holidayDates.ts` |
| Colour families (15, five colours each) and the festival themes | `src/features/create/domain/templateTheme.ts` |
| Card type, label, greeting, headline, effect, story default, openers | `types.ts`, `templates.ts`, `audienceOccasion.ts`, `occasionEffects.ts`, `experienceMode.ts`, `messageStarters.ts` |
| Starting a card | `useCreateDraft.startFromHoliday` |
| Screens | `HolidaysScreen`, `HolidayRow`, and `UpcomingHolidays` (the row at the top of `CreateHomeScreen` and `OccasionScreen`, fed by `comingUpHolidays`) |
| The recipient's page | `docs-site/src/lib/holidays.ts` (twin of the list), `recipientCard.ts`, `templateThemes.ts`, `occasionEffects.ts`, `experience/momentTheme.ts` (story look built from the colour family), `storyCopy.ts`, `resolveExperience.ts` |

The recipient's page cannot import from the app, so it keeps a copy of the table.
`__tests__/create/holidayParity.test.ts` fails when the two differ (names, greetings, colours, effects, story
default), so a change to one that is not made to the other cannot ship.

## Adding a festival

1. Add one entry to `DEFS` in `holidays.ts` (label, group, colour family, frame occasion, effect, icon, date rule,
   cue, opener). A moon festival lists 2026 to 2030 in order.
2. Add its twin line to `WEB_HOLIDAYS` in `docs-site/src/lib/holidays.ts`. The parity test names what is missing.
3. Run the tests. Nothing else needs to change: the list, Home, the card, the story and the preview pick it up.

A new colour family is a line in `templateTheme.ts` (`LOOKS`) and the same line, plus its story look
(effect, backdrop, costume, pattern), in `docs-site/src/lib/holidays.ts` (`HOLIDAY_LOOKS`).

## Dates: what is worked out and what is listed

- **Worked out:** fixed days (Christmas, Independence Day), nth weekdays (Mother's Day, Father's Day, Friendship
  Day, Thanksgiving) and Western Easter. These are right for any year.
- **Listed:** festivals that follow the moon (Diwali, Holi, Raksha Bandhan, Eid, Navratri, Lunar New Year,
  Hanukkah and the rest). No formula gives the day people keep, so each has a written list for 2026 to 2030.
  They are the India calendar days from the open-source `holidays` data (vacanza/holidays, MIT), checked
  against other published calendars. Most agree. Janmashtami 2027 is written as 25 Aug, and some calendars give
  24 Aug (a rule about midnight). A moon festival can fall a day apart depending on where someone lives, so the
  list says so under the last row.
- **Running out:** after 2030 a moon festival has no day; it shows "Date to be added" and Home stops suggesting
  it, but its card still works. `holidays.test.ts` fails once the lists no longer cover next year, which is the
  reminder to extend them (add a year to each `listed(...)` and raise `LISTED_LAST_YEAR`).
- Hanukkah and Rosh Hashanah are the first full day, not the evening before.

## Not done yet

- **Festival artwork** is now built (diyas, crackers, Santa and a tree, pumpkins and more for all 51; see
  [festival-art-and-coach.md](festival-art-and-coach.md)). Still plain: the 3D gift wrap, the link-preview image and Pip's face.
- **Region.** Everyone sees the same list. Home suggests only the widely celebrated festivals, so an Indian
  account is not told about Thanksgiving, but a US account is not told about Diwali either. A region or language
  setting would let Home choose better.
- **Saved people.** The Vault's reminders and auto-send are for birthdays and anniversaries. Sending a festival
  wish to everyone saved (with the same consent and yearly cap as WhatsApp) is the natural next step.
- **Festival frames.** The 18 photo frames are the same for every moment. A few festival-specific frames
  (a diya border, a rangoli corner) would make the cards look more particular.
- **Other languages.** Greetings are English, with the well-known native ones where the festival is known by
  them (Eid Mubarak, Ramadan Mubarak, Shana Tova, Saal Mubarak).
- **Not seen on a phone.** The new Home card and list were type-checked, tested and exercised in tests, but not
  looked at on a device: the dev build cannot sign in on the simulator until it is rebuilt. The recipient's
  pages and previews were looked at in the browser.
